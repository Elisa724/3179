OVERVIEW: LIVING COST CRISIS

When Family Budgets Stretch Thin

Like the balloon on the right, Australian household budgets have been pumped close to their breaking point since 2021.

Even as headline inflation cools in 2026, slower inflation only means prices stop climbing as fast. Everyday bills never went back down, leaving families to navigate a permanently higher cost of living.

KEY TAKEAWAY: CITIES & ESSENTIALS

Why Families Cannot Simply Cut Back

While price hotspots shifted south from Brisbane in 2025 to Hobart (4.5%) in 2026, the real concern for households is what is driving the bill at the checkout.

Across every state on the left, unavoidable needs like rent, power, and groceries are rising much faster than optional spending.

In Adelaide, essentials jumped 5.9% while optional items rose just 3.0%. Families can easily skip a holiday, but they cannot opt out of paying for food or electricity.

KEY TAKEAWAY: HOUSEHOLD BASKET

Relief at the Bowser, Pain at the Clinic

Half of every dollar a typical family spends (50.3%) is locked into just three basics: housing, food, and transport. When one bill cools down, another quickly takes its place.

As global oil prices settled, transport tumbled from the fastest growing cost (rank #1 in 2021) down to the lowest (rank #7 in 2026), giving drivers welcome relief at the petrol pump.

Yet families barely felt those savings because health climbed to rank #1 (2.2%), as higher insurance and doctor fees replaced fuel as the main budget headache.

KEY TAKEAWAY: WAGES VS. PRICES

Working Harder for a Smaller Paycheck

Supermarket prices can jump overnight, but workplace pay deals take years to renegotiate. For three straight years, Australian workers watched their paychecks buy less every month, with real buying power dropping 3.4% in South Australia at the 2023 peak.

It took until 2024 for overdue pay rises in essential sectors like Healthcare (6.2%) and cooling inflation to finally stop the slide, bringing state wages back near balance by 2026.

KEY TAKEAWAY: RATES & HOME LOANS

Locked Out of the Housing Market

To cool down 7.9% peak inflation, the Reserve Bank had to raise interest rates fast. While higher rates slowed down spending, they also shrank how much money banks would lend to young buyers.

For aspiring homeowners in expensive cities, the door slammed shut. New first home buyer loans in Victoria plunged by 32.8% (falling by over $2 billion a quarter) as buyers were priced out of getting a foot on the property ladder.

KEY TAKEAWAY: THE RATE CLIFF & PERSONAL DEBT

Tipping the Scales: When Debt Outweighs Income

Families who locked in cheap pandemic mortgages faced a harsh shock when those deals expired. Homebuyer Fixed rates more than tripled from 2.19% to 6.74%, adding hundreds of dollars to monthly repayments overnight.

With higher mortgage bills draining their savings, everyday households had no buffer left for car repairs or big family costs.

Like the tipping scale on the right, piling General Personal Loans ($5.17B, up more than 3x) on top of heavy home loans has pushed household debt completely out of balance.
