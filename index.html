OVERVIEW: LIVING COST CRISIS

The Price Pressure Squeeze

Between 2021 and 2026, supply shocks and strong housing demand pushed Australian living costs to multi-decade highs.

Like a balloon stretched to its limit, family budgets grew thin. Even as headline inflation cools, high everyday prices still weigh heavily across the nation.

KEY TAKEAWAY: CITIES & ESSENTIALS

Shifting Hotspots and the Everyday Squeeze

As shown in the side-by-side maps above, national price pressure shifted south over time. Brisbane (4.8%) was the clear hotspot in July 2025, but by July 2026 Hobart (4.5%) and Adelaide (4.4%) recorded the steepest inflation rates.

Despite regional shifts, the bar chart on the left shows one consistent trend across every state: basic necessities like groceries, rent, and power rose much faster than optional spending.

This divide is widest in Adelaide, where essential items climbed by 5.9% compared to just 3.0% for discretionary goods, leaving households unable to avoid higher weekly bills.

KEY TAKEAWAY: HOUSEHOLD BASKET

Shifting Costs: Fuel Relief vs. Rising Service Bills

Half of a typical family budget goes straight into three basic needs: housing (21.4%), food (17.4%), and transport (11.5%). Because families cannot skip rent or groceries, price hikes here leave little room to save.

In the rank chart below, transport dropped from rank #1 in 2021 to rank #11 (-0.7%) in 2026 as global fuel prices cooled and government energy rebates took effect.

However, savings at the bowser were offset by rising service bills. Health climbed from rank #9 in 2022 to rank #1 in 2026 (2.2%) driven by higher health insurance and medical fees.

KEY TAKEAWAY: WAGES VS. PRICES

The Wage-Price Trap: Why Paychecks Fell Behind

In 2021, private sector pay rises stayed low at 1.0% to 1.6% while living costs jumped, cutting purchasing power in the Northern Territory (-4.3%) and Queensland (-3.4%).

The squeeze peaked in 2023 as real wages fell over 3% in South Australia (-3.4%) and New South Wales (-3.3%). After overdue pay rises in Healthcare (6.2%) in 2024, inflation cooled to 3.9% by 2026, bringing Victoria (-0.3%) and Western Australia (-0.2%) back near balance.

KEY TAKEAWAY: RATES & HOME LOANS

How High Inflation Froze First Home Buying

When national inflation peaked at 7.9% in late 2022 (sitting 4.9% above the safe target limit), the Reserve Bank rapidly lifted the cash rate from 0.10% to 4.35%.

Higher borrowing costs shrank loan budgets across every state by 2023. Victoria recorded the steepest drop of 32.8% (falling from $6.4B to $4.3B), while New South Wales fell by 24.6% (from $5.7B to $4.3B).

KEY TAKEAWAY: THE RATE CLIFF & PERSONAL DEBT

The Fixed-Rate Cliff and the Personal Credit Surge

Homeowners rolling off low pandemic mortgages faced a steep repayment jump. Owner-Occupier 3-Year Fixed rates rose by +4.55 percentage points, climbing from 2.19% in 2021 to 6.74% in 2026.

Standard variable mortgage rates followed closely behind, jumping by +4.25 percentage points from 4.52% up to 8.77% and draining family savings buffers across the country.

To cover big-ticket costs and manage debt, households turned to personal credit. In the streamgraph below, quarterly General Personal Loans more than tripled from $1.63B to $5.17B, while total loan refinancing nearly doubled from $925M to $1.76B.
